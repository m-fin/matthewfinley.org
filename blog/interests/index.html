Bots, robots, drones, and Bitcoin

There is a difference between a project and an interest, and this site is trying to keep the difference visible. SKYCHARGE, DRIFTHOME, Jingle, Lake House Manager, and sonicrift are projects. They have names and, in most cases, a URL. AI bots, robots, drones, and Bitcoin are interests. They are the subjects I keep returning to while the degree still has about a year and a half on it.

AI bots, for me, means software that can hold a task and use tools, not a promise that I am shipping an assistant. Robots and drones are the physical version of the same curiosity: machines that move because software told them to. Bitcoin is the money-and-protocol version: a system you can run, read, and, if you want, pay someone with. The support page has a slot for a Bitcoin address and a Lightning address for that last reason. Both slots are empty until I put a real address in the config. An interest in Bitcoin is not a wallet printed on a website.

I am not announcing a lab, a fund, a fleet, or a startup around these four words. Cary, NC is the place I am, and the work that is far enough along is the work already linked. If a drone project or a bot becomes a public repository, it will show up in the GitHub list on its own, and I can write about it with the same restraint I am using for the games: what it is, where the link is, and what is still coming soon.

The internship at RIA Advisory sits in a different drawer. June through August 2021, full stack consultant intern. That was a job. It is part of the biography on the about page, and it is not one of these interests. I mention it in the same note only to keep the timeline honest. School is the current chapter. The interests are what I expect to still care about when the remaining year and a half is over.

If you want to talk about any of this, the address is hello@matthewfinley.org. I would rather get a specific question than a generic offer. The guestbook on the contact page is for short public notes, and those notes stay hidden until I approve them, which is a small example of the bot problem: a public form without a check fills up with nonsense. Turnstile is the check, once it is configured.
